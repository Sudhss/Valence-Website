text_buffer.cpp the document model

      
One string per line.

      
Valence stores a document as std::vector<std::string>. No rope, no piece table. Finding a line is O(1), which the renderer needs dozens of times a frame. Edits pay the price: typing into a line shifts the rest of that line, and splitting a line shifts every line below it. Type in the editor. The right side is the vector, and each edit shows what it moved.

undo_manager.cpp the command pattern

        
Ctrl+Z undoes words, not characters.

        
Every edit is recorded as an action. Single-character edits merge into one group while they are the same kind, next to each other, on the same side of a word boundary, and at most 400 ms apart. Anything else opens a new group. The towers are the undo and redo stacks, live.

cpp_highlighter.cpp the tokenizer

      
Every character goes somewhere.

      
One pass, left to right, no regex, no backtracking. The scan head crosses the line once; each time it closes a token, that token's characters leave for the lane of their type. These are Valence's rules, ported branch for branch, down to the one that knows 1'000'000'007 is a number and not a character literal.

    judge.cpp competitive programming
    
      
    Compile once. Run every case. Never freeze.
    
      
    Ctrl+Enter saves, compiles with g++ -O2 -std=gnu++17, and pipes every test case through the binary: AC, WA, TLE, RE or CE. A runaway solution is killed at the time limit and the editor never blocks. These are real runs, recorded and replayed at their real speed. The verdicts are worked out in your browser from the recorded output, by the judge's own comparison rule.

      Timed in C++ against Valence's real TextBuffer, UndoManager and CppHighlighter: 26 September 2026, g++ 13.1 -O2, Windows x64. Before V3, every keystroke re-tokenized the whole file to track block comments, and at 50,000 lines that alone overran the frame.
